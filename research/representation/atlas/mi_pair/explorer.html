How the band and the p-values are made

Surrogate. One joint multivariate IAAFT draw of the window's 32 normal-scored channels (mtsspi.surrogates.multivariate_iaaft, final step on the spectrum). It keeps every channel's power spectrum and every cross-spectrum (so each pair's linear correlation and lag structure), with marginals approximately Gaussian, and randomises everything else. It is a linear Gaussian null for the whole 32-channel system, not a per-pair one. G and K are recomputed on it for all 496 pairs.

Band. Fit an ordinary least-squares line to the surrogate's 496 (G, K) points; the half-width is the 95th percentile of their absolute residuals. The band is drawn at that width about the recording's own line, so a level shift of K (to which z is blind) cannot move points off it. The width is constant in G; the surrogate residual SD is roughly flat in G (it shrinks slightly at strong pairs under KT and PF, where the band is then conservative).

Per-pair p. A pair's residual from the recording's line is ranked against surrogate residuals: p = (1 + #{|surrogate| ≥ |pair|}) / (n + 1). With one draw, n = 496 and p cannot go below .002: Bonferroni over 496 pairs can never pass, and Benjamini–Hochberg only when many pairs sit at that floor. The default pools the surrogate residuals of every window of the same session and state (10–30 windows, so n = 4,960–14,880; each residual about its own surrogate line), assuming the null scatter is stable there: the band half-width varies by a median 5% (90th percentile 10%) across those windows. Pooling also assumes pairs are exchangeable under the null. Across the 112 session × state cells with fixed electrodes, pair identity explains 0.2–3% of the surrogate residual's variance (median ICC by state, most under drug), and a pair's surrogate residual correlates with its real residual in the same window at a median .01–.08. A per-pair null from many draws per window would therefore change little, and with 200 draws its p could not go below .005, coarser than the pooled null.

Read with care. (1) Pairs below the band are not dependence: with normal-scored marginals true MI ≥ G, and per-pair K has a sampling SD of about .05 at 1,000 samples plus a small downward bias at weak pairs under drug spectra (ksg-bias-result.md). pabove picks out pairs with more kNN MI than the line predicts. (2) The baseline is not 5% off band. Over all 1,260 windows, the mean share of pairs off band is 6.8% in eyes-open and in eyes-closed wake, 7.7% in sleep and 12.6% under drug. (3) The fall in z is broad: dropping the top 1% or 5% of pairs that carry it leaves it at least as large (drop test, edges-result.md). The most deviant pairs are worth looking at, but they are not where Δz comes from, and they mostly do not persist: pair identity explains 1–8.5% of the real residual's variance across a session's windows of one state; the histogram shows the whole cloud widening.
