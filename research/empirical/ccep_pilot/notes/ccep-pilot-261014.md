## Interpretation and sampling assessment

Uniform shared-dyad sampling does not systematically favour a channel group, but finite-sample correlation bias and large error for rare/high-leverage dyads remain possible. Concurrency changes compute time, not the sample or its sampling error; it can enable a larger budget. For M306, four nodes have 1,214 incident dyads (2.60% of 46,665), whereas their six internal dyads are only .013%. A uniform 2,500-dyad sample expects 65 incident dyads but has a 71.86% hypergeometric probability of missing all six internal dyads. These are different sampling risks. Do not assume sensor-space MEG has no localized informative subnetwork. A later computational comparison should use these full tables as references, inspect selected-coordinate errors across independent samples and group-edge coverage, then test actual sampled extraction with identical samples across worker counts. Oversampling anatomically selected groups without design-aware weighting changes the estimand. Full tables are the pilot's authorised default.

Produce one lean notebook with dimensions/QC, sampling fidelity, heatmaps, repeated-window geometry, top PC1 loading families and a few original-edge contrasts. PCA is exploratory and participant-balanced; no independent-window significance claims. Preserve native-rate responses, exclude stimulation artefacts and compare latency dispersion/reciprocity only where stimulation coverage and quality permit matching the baseline channels. Author pipeline: [mnl_ccepAge](https://github.com/MultimodalNeuroimagingLab/mnl_ccepAge); [primary study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01272-0). N1 latency is a response phenotype, not direct ground truth for spontaneous conduction delays.

## Investigation and one latency test

Planned 14 October 2026; exploratory, not a preregistered result. The three steps are explicit: (1) validate all 19 pilot outputs, fit PCA to one first retained window per participant, and inspect large loadings including non-correlation families; (2) explain a few coordinates using original edge profiles, repeated windows, reference controls and channel-deletion sensitivity; (3) test one independently motivated physiological contrast, fixed before inspecting its association with N1. A large PC1 loading is not itself a mechanistic explanation. The original five participants establish feasibility; the 19 additional participants can support subsequent evaluation only after response matching and coverage checks. Extra runs are repeated measurements.

**Primary candidate:** agreement across edges between squared zero-lag correlation, Aᵢⱼ=rᵢⱼ(0)², and squared short-lag maximum correlation, Bᵢⱼ=max|ℓ|≤12 rᵢⱼ(ℓ)², at the frozen 128 Hz/0.5–45 Hz resolution. Define z_short=corr_edges(A,B), using unique undirected edges, demeaned signals and the same fixed-T normalization at every lag. The ±12-sample window is ±93.75 ms; no thresholding or tuning against N1. This is a small diagnostic computed from prepared arrays, not an existing p90 coordinate or a change to pyspi/the running corpus. Frozen fd0de71 p90 CrossCorrelation searches ±T/4=±8 s, which cannot simply be labelled a millisecond-delay contrast. Existing `corr_pearson_tau-1 × corr_pearson_tau-10` (7.8125 versus 78.125 ms) is a related descriptive contrast, but is also sensitive to spectral/oscillatory organisation; do not choose between candidates by the better N1 association.

**Hypothesis and calibration:** lower z_short means allowing short lags changes the relative pattern of strong edges. Heterogeneous delays in shared components can cause this and could accompany broader evoked arrival times; neither a universal negative association nor identification of conduction delays follows. Before N1 association, use controlled channel shifts within the fixed lag range, with spectra preserved and varied coupling/noise, to check that the coordinate distinguishes delay heterogeneity from simple changes in overall strength. Include equal-delay/no-change controls and inspect original correlation-versus-lag curves. If the explanation fails or either edge profile is effectively constant, stop the latency interpretation rather than tuning the lag window. The 7.8125 ms baseline grid and 45 Hz bandwidth limit temporal specificity; N1 measurement remains native-rate.

**Matched unit:** for each already eligible stimulation site, take detected N1 receiver contacts also present in the baseline, excluding the stimulating contacts. Estimate baseline z_short among those receiver contacts and N1 latency IQR across the same receivers. This matches relative receiver timing to receiver–receiver organisation instead of correlating a whole-brain descriptor with an unrelated set of stimulated edges. Detection defines the target population: detected responses only; report response fraction and do not treat nondetections as zero latency. Select sites with at least eight detected receivers, repeatedly take eight without replacement with seed261014, and use the identical subset for baseline and response summaries. Eight is a feasibility choice (28 unique edges), not a precision guarantee. Current pilot eligibility before anatomical matching is 1/12/25/20/3 sites for participants02/10/12/61/73; estimates remain participant-level, not 61 independent sites.

**Coverage and reference:** first inspect receiver Destrieux labels, stimulation-to-receiver and receiver-pair distance distributions, without selecting on z or latency values. Predefine coverage-matched subsets on shared anatomical/distance support (20 mm distance bins); report exclusions and effective site/contact counts. If overlap cannot retain eight receivers per site in at least four participants, the coverage-controlled between-participant test is not feasible in this pilot; retain illustrative within-site results only. Available coordinates are fsaverage-projected millimetres, so distances are approximate spatial controls, not tract lengths. Use the same contact masks under acquisition reference and CAR over the original full retained channel set; do not recompute CAR over each eight-contact subset. Median across sites gives one equal-weight participant summary; repeated baseline windows supply sensitivity ranges. Report the descriptive participant-level rank association and leave-one-participant-out direction, without treating resamples/windows/edges as independent. Check mean constituent strengths, spectrum and seizure-onset-zone contact exclusion as explanations; five participants cannot support a multivariable adjusted claim.

**Positive result:** the chosen contrast has an explained edge-level origin, is reproducible across windows, retains its association direction under reference/coverage sensitivity and is not driven by one participant. A plausible scatterplot alone is insufficient. N1's relationship to anatomy/distance motivates the controls, but the proposed baseline contrast is an inference to test, not a finding from the [primary study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01272-0). Reference effects are also material for CCEP interpretation ([CARLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00185)); this test changes baseline referencing only and does not claim published N1 derivatives are reference-independent.
