## Strength as a per-recording nuisance on proof classes, 2026-10-06

Question from the user: can coupling strength be normalised per MTS so that mean |r| and per-SPI means become noise while z keeps structure. Run `proof-strength-nuisance-261006` (full p90, M16/T1000): eight coupled classes (ring VARs .2/.39 and .7/.14 at radius .98, Kuramoto K=3.5, wave, CML frozen-chaos/sti-i/defect-turbulence/fdstc) plus unmodulated Gaussian/Cauchy noise, 40 realizations, each observed in five arms through homogeneous white sensor noise: native; equalised (mean |r|=.14); mild (η~U[.3,.9]); matched (mean |r|~U[.08,.20], same in every class); wide (attenuation U[.25,.95]). Fresh-seed repeat of native/matched at M24. 2,400 records, zero failures. Executed [notebook](../../research/proof/baselines/notebooks/proof_strength_nuisance_261006.ipynb); code `research/proof/baselines/proof_strength_nuisance.py, research/proof/baselines/build_proof_strength_notebook.py`; unsupervised fits per arm and panel with the proof preprocessing.

- **Equalising fails again; varying works partly.** Equalised strength leaves standardized means as clustered as native (CML silhouette .44 vs .43; across classes means .50 beat z .41). Strength that varies between recordings spreads the means along it: CML silhouette of means .26 (matched) and .13 (wide) against z .52 and .51; M24 matched .32 against .61. The mean UMAP then loses separate CML clusters and z keeps four. The CML ordering survives interquartile and rank-Gaussian scaling of the means (wide: .14, .24), unit-variance z (.34) and four UMAP seeds. This is the closest result yet to the requested picture, and it is a geometry result only.
- **Means are never noise.** Five-neighbour class agreement and grouped logistic accuracy stay ≥.98 for means and z in every arm and both panels; dropping the mean vector's PC1 does not restore compactness (.12). Across families the mean UMAP still separates classes. Mean |r| alone is at chance when matched (.31; chance .25).
- **z is less strength-sensitive, not blind.** Within a class (wide arm), 80% of SPI means have |Spearman| with mean |r| above .5 against 26% of z coordinates (medians .73 vs .40). Under heavy noise the two VARs and Kuramoto merge in two dimensions for both representations.
- **Qualifications.** Within CML z is already more compact natively (gap .21; .25 matched; .38 wide; M24 .25 to .28), so only the growth is attributable to strength. Sensor noise only attenuates: fdstc (native mean |r| .13 at M16, .10 at M24) is left as generated in 55%/78% of matched recordings. Equalised and matched put more noise on natively stronger classes; mild and wide do not. One nuisance, one length.
- **Design lessons.** Exact matching needs native strength above the target range; ring systems dilute mean |r| with M; a 13-statistic local probe run agreed with p90 in direction and was not used as evidence. Kuramoto at K=-4 (the proof setting, native .20) is too weak for these targets, hence K=3.5.
- Identity worth stating in any write-up: standardising each SPI's profile within a recording zeroes every mean and leaves z exactly unchanged, so z is by construction what remains after removing the set of strengths.
