## Question and representation policy

Test whether an unlabeled SPI–SPI coordinate changes with an independently defined physical order parameter or dynamical-regime diagnostic as a control is swept. The goal is across-control tracking; no longitudinal extension is required.

- Run the complete `configs/pyspi/benchmarked_p90.yaml`; do not intentionally exclude an SPI family. Target-blind, development-only validity handling, imputation, variance filtering, centring and reduction are allowed.
- For MPI edge vectors `v_a`, `z_ab=corr(v_a,v_b)` is a correlation-Gram geometry of estimator response profiles over channel pairs. It loses each MPI's affine level/scale and dyad labels, but an informative MPI can affect every pair containing it, so the representation is not blind to individual-SPI information. The current contract uses all M(M−1) ordered off-diagonal entries (user decision 2026-10-21; [cross-M,T context](cross-mt-transfer.md)).
- Call `q` unsupervised only if every representation and hyperparameter choice is target/control blind; a later `q -> Q` calibration is supervised. Display signs may use development Q for orientation only.
- New dependence-transition examples need at least 16 observed processes, preferably M=N; intrinsically low-dimensional systems such as the six-state Rössler pair are explicit exceptions. Never pad or duplicate channels. This is a design preference, not a guarantee of reliable SPI–SPI estimation.
- Analysis contract: (1) reproduce the physics without pyspi, establishing burn-in, stationarity, finite-size/time convergence and independent seeds; (2) freeze controls, Q, observation, development and untouched confirmation masters, then run full p90 on Gadi; (3) fit hygiene and reduction on development rows only, using PC1 only if target-blind one-dimensionality and leave-group stability pass (curved geometry may use a frozen PCA(d)-to-manifold map chosen by eigengap, connectivity and stability, never Q; branching or hysteretic planes need at least 2-D or branch-specific coordinates); (4) report held q–Q and within-control association against raw/simple, control-only and individual-SPI baselines, with a supervised decoder only if numerical inference is claimed.
- M,T transfer keeps physical N fixed, takes nested channel views and prefixes from many independent maximal trajectories, and applies one development-fitted mask, scale and embedding. Separately fitted coordinates are not comparable; nested views are paired sensitivities, not replicates. Start with M={8,16,32}; SL is the full M × T robustness system, while MH and quadratic CML used T=1000 only. Default T={100,500,1000}: fit on T≥500 and apply to T=100 as a stress cell; T=2000 is not added because p90 validity gains from 1000 to 2000 are small. Full observation and sample-length contract: [benchmark source report](../../research/inference/benchmarks/notes/report-source.md).
- Claim-bearing sweeps are one-dimensional. A cheap two-control physics map may validate an intercept (done for SL); a full control × M × T × instance design is justified only if no one-dimensional path separates the regimes.
- All-bank maximum-missingness gates were too brittle in both the MH and SL confirmations: a few near-constant views invalidated hundreds of clean rows. Do not rewrite them retrospectively; future preregistration specifies target-blind per-row exclusion, a maximum excluded fraction and minimum retained coverage per control/M/T cell.
- The EVR-ratio ≥1.5 screen (inherited from CML protocol d619b02 and finite pipeline 936dfb7) is a target-blind workflow heuristic, not a physical requirement, calibrated test or necessary condition for q/Q association. Keep failed verdicts, but do not equate failure with absence of tracking.
- Figures: control versus physical Q and sign-oriented q with independent-seed uncertainty; q-versus-Q scatter is optional and any numerical calibration is supervised. Two-control claims need Q/boundary and frozen-q maps. Equation/control/Q and robust per-process icefire MTS snapshots are orientation, not evidence. Keep the one-control cuts: MH varies g; SL varies gamma at K=.8; quadratic CML varies alpha at eps=.3, crossing finite-time reorganizations rather than precise asymptotic phase lines.

## Where the evidence stands

q tracks physical Q in every promoted canonical system (held |rho| about .75–.95), but on rank association simple statistics (mean |r|, mean-SPI PC1, spectral entropy, phase coherence, channel or input SD) match or beat it in each of them. The supported claim is order-coordinate sensitivity and unsupervised accessibility, not superiority, numerical calibration or within-control fluctuation recovery. The only demonstrated baseline advantage is an ordering-of-variance effect under recording-specific sensor noise in 2:1 locking; it fails when the nuisance changes dependence type, and real NeuroTycho induction reverses it.

### Cross-frequency 2:1 locking

- [Protocol, results, lessons and open decisions](../../research/inference/locking/notes/cross-frequency-locking-261006.md). Published 2:1 resonant two-community model plus a non-resonant reference community, M=N=24, cross-coupling the only control. Code `research/inference/locking/cross_frequency_locking{,_snr}.py`; results `results/order-parameter-inference/cross-frequency-locking{,-snr,-nuisance}-261006/`.
- Clean recordings: mean |r| is flat through locking, every catalogue PC1 steps, and z-PC1 has no advantage over mean-SPI PC1. Recording-specific sensor noise: mean |r| and mean-SPI PC1 follow the noise, and held |rho| with Q for centered z-PC1 / mean-|r| / mean-SPI PC1 is .871/.032/.020 at M24 and .855/.133/.115 at M48. Standardized z-PC1 is contaminated. The advantage is an ordering of variance, not an invariance: the transition is the second mean-SPI component (.76) and the noise the second z component.
- Nuisance arms (fresh seeds, full p90; z-PC1 versus mean-SPI PC1): sensor noise .88 vs .16 at M24 and .86 vs .12 at M48; internal-coupling heterogeneity .85 vs .61 and .89 vs .53; a shared common-mode signal defeats z-PC1 (.09 at M24; .06 vs .12 at M48) because it changes the mix of instantaneous and lagged dependence, not its scale. Claim: robust to rescaling nuisances, not to ones that change dependence type. Common mode is a shared observation signal, not a common node; variable internal coupling changes the physical Q/boundary.
- NeuroTycho anaesthetic induction (15 dates, 2,239 windows, `research/representation/neurotycho/neurotycho_induction.py`) reverses the outcome: mean |r| and mean-SPI PC1 separate awake from anaesthetized on held animals (AUROC .96–1.00) with a clean transition at injection, while centered z-PC1 separates animals (.70–.72), because the real transition is strength-dominated.
- Interpretation scope is varying independent sensor noise at M24/T1000. One PC1 loading vector is fitted to all 168 development records; eight leave-seed-out ranges are sensitivity diagnostics, not CIs (leading weight −.05038, range −.05104 to −.04966), and feature-curve bands show held-instance variation in z, not weight uncertainty. Centered PC1 is kept throughout (standardized is better on clean rank, worse under variable noise; no per-panel winner). Noise eta is one amplitude per recording shared across channels, with independent realizations per channel and time. Exact Q uses arg(Y)−arg(X2), approximately arg(Y)−2arg(X1).
- Feature ranking (`research/inference/benchmarks/spi_feature_gallery.py`; evidence `results/order-parameter-inference/locking-features-m24-261009/`): the replayed PC1 (max error 1.95e-8, held Q rho .871) ranks 25,417 eligible coordinates. The top 16 by |PC1 weight| and by |development Spearman with Q| do not overlap: PCA weights measure covariance with PC1, not Q association, and target-ranked coordinates are explanatory, not a new readout. Largest weight: lag-2 Spearman × squared lag-20 Kendall (w −.0504, held rho −.895, development SD .477); top Q association: squared lag-4 Pearson × distance correlation (w .0060, held rho .952, SD .052).
- The top 20 weights are all lagged variants, keep their signs in all eight leave-seed refits and have aligned rho ≥.80 in every held-seed sweep; minimum full loading cosine .9894, maximum pooled |rho(feature, eta)| .101, together 3.93% of squared loading norm. Strong evidence against a few-record artefact, not generalization; redundancy and sensitivity to frequency and sample interval remain.
- Composition (coefficient-distribution summaries, not variance or information attribution): classical-correlation coordinates (lagged/cross-correlation, rank correlations, squares, and empirical covariance as Pearson-equivalent on standardized channels; excluding other covariance/precision, distance/envelope correlation and coherence) number 1,431 (5.63%), take 45 of the top 100 (median global rank 5,812) and 23.06% of squared loading norm (all covariance included: 2,264 and 25.86%; filtered top 20 unchanged). Squared norm splits 23.06% two correlation endpoints, 42.67% exactly one, 34.27% neither (45/23/32 of the top 100); coordinates touching Johansen or TE each take about 15.7%, overlapping. Lag 20 appears in 68 of the top 100 versus lag 2 in 18, but lag 20 is the configured maximum, not an established optimum or physical delay. The highest features with at most one correlation endpoint start at rank 26: Johansen across lag/trend settings, lag-20 × one-step conditional entropy/MI, and LCSS × TE. `xme_*_k1` is H(Y_next|X_current); Gaussian xme/TLMI are close to squared lag-1 Pearson, so they are not independent nonlinear evidence, and identical LCSS variants are not separate discoveries.
- Retrospective family removal (`locking-preview-261007/`; replay error <5.1e-8, development .08→.12 ranking): without lagged-correlation coordinates, sensor-noise PC1 follows the noise (Q rho .041 versus full .855; temporal-mean PC1 .065); internal-coupling reduced z gives .801 versus full .886; the common-mode failure persists. Strong non-lagged coordinates do not guarantee reduced-PC1 success. The top four redundant lagged pairs contribute .62% of absolute change, all coordinates touching lagged correlation about 62%.
- Lag mechanism (`mechanistic_lag_audit()`, all 168 held records, rank-7 Pearson-lag2 × squared-Pearson-lag20): six community-block means preserve z (RMSE .001844, rho .996892). From gamma 0 to .14, within-group lag20/lag0 moves A −.835→.006, B −.536→−.994, C .422→.418: within-community delayed dependence is redistributed as frequencies pull into locking, which is not a direct measurement of nonlinear A–B coupling. The post-hoc group-labelled contrast D=(mean_B(r20²)−mean_A(r20²))/(sum) moves −.418→1.000 with rho(D,Q) .906 and rho(D,eta) −.066 (this z: −.891 and −.022); it is explanatory and system-specific, and does not establish necessity for all of PC1 or separate locking from frequency-matched alternatives.
- Johansen (`mechanistic_johansen_audit()`, rank 26, three held records at gamma 0/.10/.14 nearest eta .6, no Q selection): z changes −.493/−.152/+.080; short-history trace favours fast-B pairs, long-history max-eigen initially favours A pairs, and the disagreement weakens. Six-block approximations (−.582/−.270/+.171) are less exact than the lag audit, and disconnected A–C statistics also change, so the signature need not identify direct coupling. Not cointegration evidence: bounded stationary oscillations and model-memory sensitivity preclude the stochastic-trend reading.
- Edge profiles (`research/inference/locking/plot_locking_m24_edge_profiles.py`; three p90 matrices SHA-verified against `cross-frequency-locking-snr-261006/sources.json`; held rows g000-s209/g100-s212/g140-s215; all 552 directed edges; 15 z replays within 1.50e-8): A–B mean TE rises .001→.064 while LCSS stays .770→.776, and kernel CE falls 1.383→1.315 while Gaussian TLMI stays near 0. Illustrative recordings with differing eta and seeds, not a population test; negative finite-sample TE is displayed, not clipped. Earlier hash-verified M48 examples likewise show A–B TE rising while Pearson and LCSS stay roughly unchanged; neither is causal-flow proof.
- Supported reading: PC1 mixes temporal phase organisation (best resolved: community redistribution of delayed dependence) with predictive-versus-waveform dependence (LCSS–TE); Johansen remains descriptive short/long-history model sensitivity. Do not call these separately recovered axes or a pure coupling-type coordinate: one mixed PC1 was fitted, redundant features influence it, and the lag mechanism can arise from within-community frequency/autocorrelation change. Established: order-coordinate accessibility beyond the named mean-based baselines; mean-PC1 failure does not imply absence of Q information in mean-SPIs.
- Cross-system interpretation hierarchy ([three-systems notebook](../../research/inference/benchmarks/notebooks/order-coordinate-three-systems.ipynb)): the hardware 2×20 ms spectral axis at matched mean Pearson is calibrated beyond that scalar, not all strength information; locking LCSS–TE is the best candidate for predictive-versus-waveform dependence; the locking lag contrast resolves temporal redistribution, but frequency pulling remains an alternative; SI–IDS robustly marks recruitment without establishing strength independence; the CML signed/absolute orthogonal-envelope reversal is mostly transformation geometry, not a separately recovered mechanism.

- Miller–Huse (mu=3, refined g_c=.20534(2), L=128 with dispersed sensors M={8,16,32}): development held rho .9125, within-control .2381; the interleaved confirmation stopped before outcomes on one row's missingness, and the sealed sensitivity excluding it gives .8885 [.8476, .9204], within .0148. - Desai–Zwanzig (Evangelou et al. 2024, sigma_c=1.890, N=12000, M=32 partial observation): held rho .9472 [.9010, .9877], control-mean .9948; q and Q share the steepest interval 1.86–1.87, .025 below the mean-field boundary. - CML2D collective period doubling (L=256, g=.2, dispersed M=32, T=1000; source arXiv nlin/0605004, microscopic chaos on both sides; [development](../../research/inference/cml/notes/cml2d-results-260911.md), [confirmation](../../research/inference/cml/notes/cml2d-confirmation-results-260911.md)): development rho .8983; independent confirmation .883018 [.867676, .898475] with zero exclusions over 1,408 rows; q and Q share the steepest interval 3.86212–3.86306. - Finite full observation ([ledger](../../research/inference/candidate_search/notes/finite-full-observation-260915.md)): Rössler six-state .848, TASEP N32 .746 (sampled density stronger, .828), native TASEP N64 .813, frozen N32→N64 transfer .782 (retrospective); L96 and Hindmarsh–Rose are weak negatives (.331/.161); FHN excluded on source-sign/scale ambiguity.
