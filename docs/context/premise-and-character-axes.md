## Background

Multivariate recordings support complementary questions about individual channels, pairwise dependence, network topology and dynamical mechanisms. Do not present these overlapping traditions as a verified linear historical progression.

## Problems

1. **A statistic selects aspects of dependence.** Covariance measures linear association without assuming the generating interaction is linear; it can respond to nonlinear dependencies. Instantaneous MI can respond to delayed interactions with instantaneous consequences. Detecting a change and identifying its dynamical explanation are distinct tasks.
2. **A selected representation can leave character ambiguous.** A scalar edge score generally does not identify a dependence relation uniquely. Existing nonlinear tests, lag estimators and multi-measure analyses already address aspects of character; the gap cannot be that all prior methods see only strength.
3. **Joint organisation is a useful target.** Separate summaries of each SPI omit which channel pairs receive jointly high or low scores across measures. SPI–SPI compresses this correspondence into a recording descriptor, with its own catalogue, preprocessing and aggregation biases. Full aligned MPI collections already contain this information.

**Aim.** Develop and validate a broadly applicable recording-level representation of cross-statistic dependence organisation, then establish when its changes admit a useful dynamical interpretation. A first/general-purpose priority claim and unique mechanistic identification are not established.

Distinguish label-free feature/embedding construction from supervised readouts and independently validated interpretation. ## What SPI–SPI is claimed to do

It asks how notions of dependence themselves covary across the channel pairs of one recording: z_ab = corr(A^(a)_ij, A^(b)_ij). Statistical identities supply common coordinates across recordings; physical interpretation requires calibration. Candidate illustrations are:

- {Pearson, Spearman}: changes in their edge-profile agreement can motivate nonlinearity hypotheses, but are not a calibrated nonlinearity score; marginal distributions, strength ranges and estimation matter.
- {Euclidean distance, cross-Euclidean distance}: profile agreement may respond to timing structure in designed examples; it is not by itself an identified delay estimate.

Current intuition studies: [`r_rho_mi_260924.ipynb`](../../research/cases/r_rho_mi/notebooks/r_rho_mi_260924.ipynb), [`pdist-euclid_dtw_260924.ipynb`](../../research/cases/dtw_euclidean/notebooks/pdist-euclid_dtw_260924.ipynb), each with 50 independent MTS per condition. Aggregating over the combinatorial space of pairs gives a signature of dependence character. Intended use: given two groups of recordings (young and old, patient and control, or an engineering or sensor analogue), say how the nature of dependence differs — more nonlinear, more variably lagged, more variable in frequency — rather than only how strong it is. Representing recordings of different M and T in one space follows from the construction; it is a benefit, not the motivation.

Pearson aggregation is a selective compression: it removes profile levels and positive scales, retains linear cross-profile correspondence and discards edge incidence. Whether a richer comparator is useful depends on the scientific target and finite-data evidence.

## Evidence boundaries to keep attached

- z is not strength-blind; strength "bleeds" in. Positive affine changes of each SPI's edge profile leave z unchanged, as do common permutations of edge entries. Removing profile levels/scales does not remove every effect of physical coupling strength.
- Per-SPI means are not strength-only. With the full catalogue, supervised mean readouts recover character targets, and the unsupervised mean-SPI PC1 matched z-PC1 on homogeneous recordings ([locking result](../../research/inference/locking/notes/cross-frequency-locking-261006.md); [ruled-out record](cross-mt-transfer-ruled-out.md)). The supported contrast is unsupervised access under per-recording nuisances that move every dependence estimate together, not absence of information. On the proof classes (2026-10-06, [record](cross-mt-transfer.md#strength-as-a-per-recording-nuisance-on-proof-classes-2026-10-06)) equalising strength left the mean vector as clustered as before; strength varying between recordings degraded its geometry, clearly only within CML, and never its class information.
- Literature check, 2026-10-19: [Cliff et al., Methods/S3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.11941v2#S3.SS1) already computes per-record absolute Spearman agreement before corpus averaging. [Liu et al. 2025, Fig. 1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-025-02704-4) compares 239 FC statistics using signed Spearman edge-profile correlations per participant, then averages across participants; it also reports between-participant/run variance. [Mostame and Sadaghiani 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.117051) directly compares phase/amplitude connectivity patterns and stimulus-related changes; [Siems and Siegel 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.116538) studies their dissociation and estimator biases. These are substantive precedents, not a minor exception. Position the advance around systematic recording representation and validated interpretation, not inventing inter-statistic correlations or retaining their signs. This targeted search is not an exhaustive priority review.
- [Theiler et al. 1992](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(92)90102-S), [Prichard and Theiler 1994](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.73.951) and [Wibral et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0055809) establish prior nonlinearity/delay approaches. [RSA](https://doi.org/10.3389/neuro.06.004.2008), [CKA](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/kornblith19a.html) and [multilayer reducibility](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms7864) are adjacent representation/layer-comparison precedents, not identical constructions. Prefer a dependence-organisation title over “mechanistic similarity”; character denotes observable statistical properties unless independently validated.
- Comparing measures to interpret dynamics also has direct precedents: [Paluš 1995](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(95)90079-9) contrasts linear/nonlinear redundancies; [Nguyen et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1103/qnx2-yp4c) identifies feature-mediated dependence. The latter's final publication is Physical Review Research 7, 043283 (10 December 2025); the bibliography's DOI TODO can be resolved with this source. Introduction recommendation: motivate dependence character, explain ambiguity of individual scores, acknowledge existing complementary probes, then introduce their joint organisation as the recording descriptor. Keep selected calibrated demonstrations central and recording-size comparability secondary. Marginal edge distributions versus joint correspondence gives a precise information-loss example; it does not establish that all network summaries or full aligned MPI collections lack character information.
- Introduction review, 2026-10-20 ([rewrite and source table](../../drafts/introduction_rewrite.md)). Most dangerous precedent: [Nolte et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2020.577574). Under Gaussianity, coupling measures are fixed functions of coherency, so part of z is set by how strength is spread across pairs. A strength-matched linear-Gaussian surrogate null is therefore required, and z minus its surrogate expectation is a candidate formal definition of non-Gaussian character. Linear lag/spectral organisation survives that null, so each finding must say which kind it is. z is a correlation across pairs: it targets character that varies across pairs, and a uniform change, such as equal slowing of every connection, mostly does not register. Motivate with heterogeneity, not uniform ageing delays. [Mitra et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.10781) is the cleanest empirical motivation: zero-lag networks are preserved across wake and slow-wave sleep while lag structure reorganises. [Baum et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1912034117) applies the same across-edge correlation between structural and functional connectivity. Targeted search found no prior per-recording representation built from the full profile of agreement between dependence statistics. That search is not exhaustive.

## Future direction: a character axis alongside strength (not started)

A joint character–strength representation is a proposed design, not an established decomposition of existing methods. Character and strength need operational definitions; neither is generally a single axis or independent of the other.

The proposal is to build named axes on [0, 1] that are directly interpretable — linear to nonlinear, fixed to variable lag, frequency variability — by fitting, per channel pair, a restricted model and a flexible one and scoring their agreement (for linearity: a Gaussian model against a flexible or learned one), then aggregating over pairs. Each axis would replace the correlation aggregator in z_ab with a model-comparison score chosen to expose one quality.

Open design questions (assistant's assessment, 2026-10-06):

- A model-agreement score must be calibrated against estimator variance, or short or noisy recordings read as "linear" by default. Established precedents exist per axis and should anchor it: surrogate-data tests and Gaussian-versus-nonparametric MI gaps for nonlinearity, lagged-versus-instantaneous decompositions for lag.
- Strength and character are not orthogonal: at weak coupling every character score is undefined in practice. The representation needs an explicit rule for pairs below a strength floor.
- Candidate further axes: instantaneous to lagged; symmetric to directed; within-frequency to cross-frequency (the locking benchmark is a ready test bed); amplitude- to phase-mediated; stationary to intermittent; pairwise to higher-order.
- Validation should reuse generators with a known character parameter before any real-data claim.
