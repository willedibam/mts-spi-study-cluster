## Sampling target and expected error

Recommended initial design: draw $b$ unordered dyads uniformly without replacement from $D=\binom M2$, retain both directions, and use those identical $2b$ positions for every SPI. This preserves direction pairing, uses a common empirical measure and yields a valid correlation-Gram construction for fully finite, nonconstant vectors. Do not independently sample positions for each SPI or each SPI–SPI coordinate.

Uniform sample moments are unbiased estimates of full-edge moments. Pearson correlation is a nonlinear ratio, so the resulting correlation estimator is generally not exactly unbiased. Under nondegenerate variances and controlled influence tails, the delta method gives roughly $\operatorname{SE}(\widehat z_{ab})\propto\sqrt{(1-b/D)/b}$; there is no universal logarithmic convergence law or elbow. A nested random path can move away from truth even while typical error decreases. This is an inference from ordinary finite-population sampling and correlation linearisation, not a universal guarantee for p90 outputs. [Finite-population sampling reference](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~stark/SticiGui/Text/estimation.htm); [Fisher-transform correlation approximation](https://online.stat.psu.edu/stat505/Lesson05).

More explicitly, standardize full-edge SPI values to $u_e,v_e$ and let $\rho$ be their full correlation. The correlation influence is $h_e=u_ev_e-\rho(u_e^2+v_e^2)/2$. For paired sampling, average h over the two orientations within each dyad. To first order the design variance is $(1-b/D)S_h^2/b$, where $S_h^2$ is the finite-dyad variance. Near-constant denominators, rare influential values and changing pair distributions can invalidate an easy fixed-budget extrapolation.

Conditional on a fixed complete MPI bank, dyads need not be physically independent for this **design-based** calculation: their values are fixed and selection is random. Dependence between directions is handled by treating the dyad as the sampling unit. Generalisation over new recordings and time-series estimation uncertainty are separate questions. Fisher's familiar $1/\sqrt{n-3}$ standard error assumes a much more specific iid setting and must not be applied literally to these paired, heterogeneous values.

A small sampled fraction alone is not a reason for a large error: once $D\gg b$, the finite-population correction is near one. Increasing M matters if it changes value heterogeneity, rare-event prevalence, conditioning or the accuracy criterion. Requiring every one of41616 coordinates to be accurate is stricter than requiring one coordinate, average z error or a stable frozen q.

Homogeneous coupling is not homogeneous pairwise data: spatial separation, collective domains and SPI-specific sparsity can produce heterogeneous edge values under identical local equations. For a periodic four-neighbour lattice, nearest-neighbour dyads are a fraction $4/(M-1)$ of all dyads. At $M=65536$, 100 uniform dyads contain only .0061 nearest-neighbour dyads in expectation (about .6% chance of any). This is a combinatorial illustration, not evidence that those pairs dominate this CML descriptor. Importance for approximating uniform all-pair z depends on statistical influence, not physical interest alone. If a spatial diagnostic later identifies a problem, distance-stratified sampling with population-weighted moments can target the same z; unweighted oversampling instead changes the descriptor. Existing exact references cover observed M up to256, not all65536 lattice sites.

Keep outer Pearson for this approximation study: outer Spearman is Pearson of ranks and defines a different target, not a robustness correction to the existing z ([NIST definition](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman1/auxillar/rankcorr.htm)). Replicate random draws quantify sampling variability; merely choosing fresh pairs does not reduce the variance of an individual draw. Whole-vector RMSE does not cancel signed coordinate errors; its smoother curves reflect aggregation and median summaries. Neither the theory nor current results establishes a universal100-dyad budget or logarithmic convergence.

## What this does—and does not—resolve about M versus N

Sampling from all physical sites targets the full-system pair distribution and avoids committing to one small induced channel subnetwork. It does **not** make every channel observed: b dyads touch at most2b distinct channels, and it does not recover the entire pair matrix. The defensible claim is approximation of a full-system descriptor, not exact full observation or recovery of every interaction.

Local structure can become rare under uniform all-pairs weighting. For the periodic four-neighbour65536-site CML, nearest-neighbour dyads constitute only $4/(65536-1)\approx.000061$ of all dyads. About49080 uniform draws are needed for a95% chance of even one such dyad (large-population approximation). This is **not** a minimum sample-size theorem for z: far pairs may carry collective information, and nearest neighbours may be irrelevant to a given coordinate. It motivates a diagnostic of distance-dependent contribution. If oversampling local pairs is useful, inverse-inclusion weighting of moments is needed to retain the original all-pairs target; unweighted local/global balancing deliberately defines a different descriptor.

Next bounded step: specify acceptable z/q error and validity coverage, then prototype exact-semantic sparse adapters on existing small full-M fixtures before an EEG-scale timing pilot. Do not infer a need for immediate65k-channel p90 or additional order-parameter generators from this assessment.
