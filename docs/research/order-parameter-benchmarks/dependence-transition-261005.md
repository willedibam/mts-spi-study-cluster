# Dependence-character transitions: exploratory scope

## Question and limits

Can an unlabeled SPI–SPI coordinate track an independently defined dynamical transition better than signed/absolute Pearson strength, and does it add information or improve access relative to all p90 SPI means? These are separate claims. Existing benchmarks establish sensitivity, not general baseline superiority. The October scalar-strength and lag-surrogate controls already show that matching Pearson or weak mean-PC1 plots do not imply weak marginal information; do not repeat them as fresh evidence.

Write $\mu_a=\langle A^{(a)}_e\rangle_e$ and $z_{ab}=\operatorname{corr}_e(A^{(a)}_e,A^{(b)}_e)$. Diverse SPI means can respond to lag, nonlinear dependence and spectra; they are not intrinsically strength-only. What z uniquely retains relative to separate marginal summaries is the aligned cross-statistic relationship over pairs. This does not establish that every dependence-type change is easier in z. Common channel permutations, or positive affine transformations of each entire MPI, cannot supply a z contrast on their own.

The old order parameters are not all literally mean Pearson: Kuramoto $R^2$ is an average phase-difference cosine (not generally Pearson of arbitrary observed waveforms); Miller–Huse magnetization and CML period doubling are distinct quantities. Nevertheless empirical success of simple baselines is sufficient to motivate the harder test. Do not overstate the algebraic equivalence.

## Candidates and decisions before new p90

| Candidate | Reference quantity and rationale | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detuned, unidirectionally coupled Rössler systems | Largest conditional response Lyapunov exponent and auxiliary-system convergence; response becomes slaved through a generally nonlinear mapping | Primary physical scout, full six-state observation |
| Rössler-driven Lorenz system | Same diagnostics, structurally different attractors; no forced equality of their coordinates | Second physical scout, full six-state observation |
| Driven logistic maps | Weak generalized synchronization before complete synchronization; analytically checkable complete-synchronization threshold | Minimal mechanism scout; eight independent two-state modules provide M=N=16, auxiliary copies excluded |
| Chimera / cluster / amplitude-death transitions | Established coherent fractions or oscillation-quenching observables, but often obvious changes in strength, marginal variance or constant channels | Reserve, not a justified all-mean-failure experiment yet; spatial rearrangement alone may be invisible to z |
| Splice or mix two attractors; monotone observation transform | Known mixing fraction is construction truth, not automatically a bifurcation; spectra, channel marginals and SPI means can reveal the change | Do not promote as physical transition. Useful as an explicitly engineered control only after checking leakage |

[Hramov and Koronovskii (2005), equations 5 and 7, Figure 1](https://arxiv.org/html/nlin/0506023) supplies the Rössler pair and driven logistic equations. The selected Rössler parameters are a=.15, p=.2, c=10, response frequency .95; compare drive frequencies1.3 and .99 around coupling .11. [Pyragas (1996)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.54.R4508) discusses weak/strong synchronization. [de Sousa Vieira and Lichtenberg (1997), equations 1–3 and 16–17](https://arxiv.org/html/chao-dyn/9709029) supplies the conditional exponents, Rössler–Lorenz equations and approximate GS onset6.6, and warns that the proposed weak/strong distinction is not universal. Do not assume a second smooth-manifold transition in the heterogeneous flow.

[Abarbanel, Rulkov and Sushchik (1996)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.53.4528) establishes the auxiliary-system diagnostic; [Rulkov et al. (1995)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.51.980) defines generalized synchronization through a functional relation rather than equality. Auxiliary replicas and tangent vectors are diagnostic machinery, not observed sensors. Feeding them to pyspi would expose the diagnostic directly and defeat the purpose.

## Implementation and evidence

Protocol: `configs/analysis/dependence-transition-261005.yaml`; implementation: `scripts/scout_dependence_transition.py`; physics/probe plot: `scripts/report_dependence_transition.py`; tests: `tests/test_dependence_transition.py`. Outputs live in `results/order-parameter-inference/dependence-transition-261005/`. The first scope uses eight initial-condition seeds, T1000 observed states, and a disjoint future reference after the observed window. RK4 integrates the full tangent response, renormalized every step; two reference halves, normalized auxiliary error, and raw scalar means remain visible. The discrete model has an independent analytic check at complete synchronization, $\lambda_\perp=\log[2(1-k)]$. Initial-condition and finite-time sensitivity are not hidden by reporting only a pooled boundary.

Three tests initially pass: response Jacobian versus finite differences, logistic Lyapunov/auxiliary/full-observation identities, and deterministic finite cheap probes. Eleven cheap pairwise probes are explicitly not p90. Primary probe q is centered PC1, standardized PC1 is a prespecified sensitivity, and the mean baseline uses standardized PC1 plus a training-ranked single coordinate. Development seeds0–3 fit PCs/rank baseline features; seeds4–7 provide exploratory validation. No target-selected principal component.

Before any claim-bearing p90 comparison, verify the physical boundary and numerical refinement, retain the original states (no outcome-driven whitening or covariance calibration), freeze the local sweep, and include training-selected single means and stronger full-mean readouts. If only scalar Pearson fails, report only that narrower result. If full means also track the transition, retain the negative comparison instead of narrowing the interval after inspecting q. No new recurring monitor or edits to polished notebooks are implied.

## Initial findings and bounded p90 decision

All 488 coarse cases completed. Longer-reference/half-step checks cover 56 cases (`physics/numerical-refinement.csv`). The close-frequency Rössler pair has positive mean CLE .00813 at coupling .09 and negative -.00285/-.00532 at .12/.14, with auxiliary convergence after longer burn. The Rössler–Lorenz reference changes sign between 6.5 (mean .0488) and 6.75 (-.0522), compatible with the published approximate onset. Individual finite-time values and auxiliary coalescence need care near the boundary.

Two important failures: strongly detuned Rössler retains substantial auxiliary error at .11/.14 despite slightly negative CLE, even with fivefold burn/reference and half step; this does not justify a single globally attracting synchronization branch. The logistic example at .33 has positive CLE approximately .007 but auxiliary trajectories coalesce exactly under floating-point arithmetic. Thus auxiliary zero alone can falsely suggest stable synchronization after long intermittent contraction; the independent tangent diagnostic must take precedence. Neither case is promoted to the initial p90 bank. This is not evidence that generalized synchronization itself is ill-defined.

The eleven-probe scout does not establish the desired all-mean failure: held absolute rho with future CLE for centered z-PC1 / standardized mean-PC1 / training-selected mean is .500/.016/.452 (detuned Rössler), .659/.346/.931 (close Rössler), .307/.800/.855 (Rössler–Lorenz), and .480/.802/.877 (logistic modules). Standardized z-PC1 is .442/.922/.628/.830, respectively. These are exploratory broad sweeps and only four validation seeds; they do not substitute for p90. The apparently favorable detuned case is not advanced merely because its comparison looks better.

A bounded full-p90 test of the two better-supported continuous-time cases is selected before any new p90 outcome: close Rössler epsilon .09–.13 and Rössler–Lorenz coupling 6.0–7.2, 13 controls each, 16 fresh initial-condition seeds each (416 M=N=6/T1000 recordings). First eight seeds train, remaining eight evaluate; fivefold burn/reference and half integration step, auxiliary states excluded. This asks whether the richer library changes the cheap-probe verdict, not whether all means have already been matched. Script `scripts/dependence_transition_pipeline.py` includes target-blind PC1s, source-ranked single means, and full-mean ridge/RBF with training-seed-grouped tuning. Keep both successful and unsuccessful outcomes; no additional phase-window selection after p90 inspection.
